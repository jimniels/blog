# No More Icons In Every Menu By Default

Have you heard the good news? [I bemoaned the approach of putting an icon in every menu item by default](https://blog.jim-nielsen.com/2025/icons-in-menus/) which shipped with macOS Tahoe — but now it’s gone in Golden Gate!

[John Gruber wrote about this reversion back in June](https://daringfireball.net/2026/06/macos_27_golden_gate_removes_the_dumb_icons_from_menu_items), including the fact that Apple’s new Human Interface Guidelines specifically call out Tahoe’s approach to menus as an example of what _not_ to do on macOS.

As the old saying goes, it takes a big ~~man~~ corporation to admit when they’re wrong.

As I mentioned in [my original post](https://blog.jim-nielsen.com/2025/icons-in-menus/), one of the arguments I had in my quiver against this practice was “Apple doesn’t do it”. But once Apple started putting icons in all their menus in Tahoe, I lost a persuasive point in my argument.

But now, I think I have an argument that’s even stronger than before!

It’s no longer: “Apple doesn’t put icons in their menus.” 

It’s: “Apple tried putting icons in all their menus, and even _they_ went back on it!”

They designed it, shipped it, lived with it for a year, then publicly changed their guidance on it. To me, that’s a lot more persuasive than simply saying, “Apple doesn’t do this.”

Choosing not to do something you’ve never done is one thing.

Choosing not to do something you’ve done is _experience_.

That’s the essence of life: [learning by doing](https://blog.jim-nielsen.com/2026/book-notes-charlie-munger/#:~:text=when%20you%20inevitably%20do%20make%20mistakes%2C%20learn%20to%20acknowledge%20them%20and%20fix%20them%20quickly).
